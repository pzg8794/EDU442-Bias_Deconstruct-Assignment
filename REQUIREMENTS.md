# Deconstructing Bias — Assignment Requirements

## Assignment identity
- **Course:** EDU 442 — Race, Class, Gender, and Disability in American Education
- **Assignment:** Deconstructing Bias
- **Due date:** **Monday, April 13, 2026**
- **Points:** **25**
- **Deliverable:** **5–7 page** paper based on **observation + reflection + citations**

## Core assignment prompt
This assignment asks you to examine the **prejudices, biases, and stereotypes** you hold about a particular **community or group**. After identifying where those biases, prejudices, or stereotypes may lie, you must **observe activity of that group or community** in an **online or in-person setting** and write a **personal reflection paper** that **critically examines** the experience.

Your paper must:
- focus on a specific **community or group**
- include an **observation** in an online or in-person setting
- critically examine your **own bias/prejudice/stereotypes**
- be **5–7 pages**
- include **citations from class readings or digital sources**

## Required thinking questions from the prompt
Your paper should address, directly or indirectly, the following:
- How will you thoughtfully connect with this group?
- What do you need to consider for your own safety and well-being?
- What current myths or assumptions exist about this group?
- What does it mean to engage with this group when you have such assumptions?
- What does it mean to engage with this group in your current or future work setting (classroom, clinic, etc.)?
- What assets or funds of knowledge did you observe?
- What can you learn from this group?
- What did you learn from this observation and reflection that will inform your future work?

## Recommended paper structure
The rubric strongly suggests organizing the paper into these sections:

1. **Introduction**
	- Identify who or what you observed.
	- Explain why you chose this group or setting.
	- State a clear **research question**.

2. **Autobiographical section**
	- Explain your identity as a raced, classed, gendered, abled, etc. person.
	- Describe how your experiences and interactions shaped you.
	- Clearly articulate the **bias and/or prejudice under investigation**.

3. **Description of the research / observation**
	- Describe who was observed.
	- Describe what activity or activities were observed.
	- Explain how the project was conducted.
	- Include details such as how many times you observed, how access was negotiated, and any issues of disclosure.
	- Richly describe the context.

4. **Reflection / analysis**
	- Carefully examine your biases.
	- Integrate course readings and class discussion meaningfully.
	- Explain the theoretical orientation you are using.
	- Articulate consequences of bias/prejudice for your work in the field.
	- Discuss your positionality, privilege, and/or marginalization in relation to equity and social justice.

5. **Mechanics / presentation**
	- Use standard academic English.
	- Use APA style consistently.
	- Include an engaging title.
	- Double-space and paginate the paper.
	- Stay within the required length.

## Rubric criteria
The paper is graded in five areas.

### 1) Introduction
To score at the highest level, the paper should:
- describe the project in detail
- clearly explain who or what was observed
- clearly explain why that choice was made
- clearly state the research question

### 2) Autobiographical section
To score at the highest level, the paper should:
- clearly explain your identity as a raced, classed, gendered, abled, etc. individual
- explain how you arrived at this place in your life
- provide a rich description of how interactions and experiences shaped you
- clearly articulate the bias and/or prejudice under investigation

### 3) Description of the research
To score at the highest level, the paper should:
- describe in detail who was observed
- describe in detail what was observed
- explain how the project was conducted
- include details such as number of observations, negotiated access, and disclosure issues
- richly describe the context for the research

### 4) Reflection
To score at the highest level, the paper should:
- clearly demonstrate careful examination of the bias under investigation
- meaningfully integrate course readings and class discussion
- describe the theoretical orientation taken
- articulate several consequences of bias/prejudice for your work in the field
- include consequences that support systemic injustice
- clearly discuss your positionality and the consequences of privilege or lack thereof on equity and social justice

### 5) Mechanics, style, and presentation
To score at the highest level, the paper should:
- be free from spelling, grammar, punctuation, syntax, and usage errors
- use APA style consistently
- include an engaging title
- be double-spaced and paginated
- have an appropriate length for the topic and audience
- conform to the assignment requirements

## What full-credit work likely looks like

- a concrete autobiographical grounding
- detailed observation notes and context
- direct engagement with your own assumptions
