Dear Future-Teacher Me,

I hope you’re tired in the good way—brain buzzing, heart full, but not completely burned out. Right now, I’m finishing the M.S. in CS with the inclusion certification and getting ready for my first full year in my own classroom. It feels like stepping into a complex system: a lot of variables, a lot of unknowns, and a deep sense that this is the right work.

*Post-discussion annotation:* Affirm/extend: Identities can feel heavy in school systems, but they can also be tools for insight, connection, and impact.

I’m excited to teach in a community—not just one-to-one—and to try the practices I’ve been building: culturally and linguistically sustaining CS lessons, trauma-informed and neurodiversity-affirming routines, and tech/AI use that actually lowers barriers instead of raising them. I’m excited to see what happens when “teacher agency as algorithmic justice” meets real constraints and real kids.

*Post-discussion annotation:* Extend: Guest teacher emphasized authenticity—students notice when you’re faking; be yourself and adapt to your students.

I’m also concerned. I worry about being swallowed by the system I want to help change: pacing guides that don’t fit the students in front of me, testing pressure, limited prep time, and policies that say “equity” but reward compliance. I worry about overwork—about becoming the kind of teacher who stops questioning because it’s easier to survive the week.

*Post-discussion annotation:* Affirm: Burnout is real; balance and boundaries are part of sustainable impact.

*Post-discussion annotation:* Challenge: Not every constraint is evil—some protect access and consistency. Learn to tell the difference between structure that supports kids and structure that silences them.

Here’s what I want to hold onto:

- Every student belongs—no “normal kid” as the standard.
- Relationship before content, especially for CLD and neurodivergent students who are often misread.
- Rigor and care are not opposites.
- Technology and AI are tools, not masters; they need to be transparent, inclusive, and just.

*Post-discussion annotation:* Challenge: “Relationship first” still needs structure—how will I keep routines firm and predictable without slipping into compliance-for-comfort?

What I feel confident about: designing tech-rich learning, spotting patterns in systems that don’t make sense, and building lessons with purpose.

Where I want to grow: pacing lessons so they actually fit in 40 minutes, setting boundaries so I don’t take everything home, and learning policy/law well enough to protect kids (and myself) when systems get it wrong.

*Post-discussion annotation:* Extend: Chaos reset idea—ask students to point to who is speaking/being loud to re-ground attention.

*Post-discussion annotation:* Challenge: If district tech isn’t transparent or inclusive, what’s my ethical line—and what workaround keeps learning accessible without quietly harming someone?

What I’m okay not knowing yet: I don’t need a perfect “management style” right now. I hope you’ve iterated toward something firm and humane. I don’t need every standard memorized; I hope you’ve built a map of what matters most and how to prioritize when the calendar gets loud.

How I’ll know we’re improving (beyond evaluations):

- Students who started guarded with adults take more academic and emotional risks with me.
- Families—especially those marginalized by schools—feel respected and communicated with, even when problems aren’t solvable overnight.
- I can name specific moments where I used policy, data, or tech to make a student’s path more humane.
- My self-talk shifts from “I’m failing” to “that didn’t work; here’s my next adjustment.”

*Post-discussion annotation:* Extend: Family communication—gather as much information as possible so conversations are grounded in data and observations, not guesses.

If it’s harder than we imagined, that doesn’t mean we failed—it might mean we’re seeing the system clearly. Stay connected to colleagues who want better, keep carving time to think and write, and keep giving yourself the compassion you try to extend to students.

Remember why we started: so it’s less likely that another CLD, neurodivergent kid slips through cracks that were never accidents.

With care,

An old, but fresh, version of you
