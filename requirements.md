## Assignment identity
- **Course:** EDU 442 — Race, Class, Gender, and Disability in American Education
- **Assignment:** Deconstructing Bias
- **Due date:** **Monday, April 13, 2026**
- **Points:** **25**
- **Deliverable:** **5–7 page** paper based on **observation + reflection + citations**

## Core assignment prompt
This assignment asks you to examine the **prejudices, biases, and stereotypes** you hold about a particular **community or group**. After identifying where those biases, prejudices, or stereotypes may lie, you must **observe activity of that group or community** in an **online or in-person setting** and write a **personal reflection paper** that **critically examines** the experience.

Your paper must:
- focus on a specific **community or group**
- include an **observation** in an online or in-person setting
- critically examine your **own bias/prejudice/stereotypes**
- be **5–7 pages**
- include **citations from class readings or digital sources**

## Required thinking questions
Your paper should address, directly or indirectly, the following:
- How will you thoughtfully connect with this group?
- What do you need to consider for your own safety and well-being?
- What current myths or assumptions exist about this group?
- What does it mean to engage with this group when you have such assumptions?
- What does it mean to engage with this group in your current or future work setting (classroom, clinic, etc.)?
- What assets or funds of knowledge did you observe?
- What can you learn from this group?
- What did you learn from this observation and reflection that will inform your future work?

## Suggested paper sections
1. **Introduction** — who/what you observed, why this group/setting, your research question.
2. **Autobiographical section** — your identity and experiences, how your bias/prejudice developed.
3. **Description of the research/observation** — who, what, where, how many times, access, disclosure, context.
4. **Reflection/analysis** — how your bias showed up, how it was challenged/complicated, how readings inform your interpretation, how this connects to equity/justice.
5. **Mechanics/presentation** — APA, length, formatting, clarity.
