## Primary Course Readings — Directly Cited in Paper

### 1. Annamma, Connor, & Ferri (2013) — DisCrit ⬅ TODAY's reading (Apr 13)
**Core claim:** Schools label, sort, and punish students through overlapping race and disability lenses. "Behavior problems" are not neutral observations — they reflect how schools produce "problem" identities at the intersection of race and (dis)ability.  
**Where used in paper:** Section 3 (Myth vs. Reality) — connect the deficit framing of students to how DisCrit shows that "difficult" labels are racialized and ableist simultaneously.  
**Key passage for paper:** Schools' disciplinary and classification systems produce disabled and racialized identities; the "problem student" is a constructed category.  
**APA:** Annamma, S. A., Connor, D., & Ferri, B. (2013). Dis/ability critical race studies (DisCrit): Theorizing at the intersections of race and dis/ability. *Race Ethnicity and Education, 16*(1), 1–31.

---

### 2. Flynn (2024) — Affirmative Non-Tragedy Model ⬅ TODAY's reading (Apr 13)
**Core claim:** Disability should be framed through an affirmative, non-tragedy lens — as a valued form of human variation, not a deficiency to remediate. This applies directly to any student whose behavior or learning pattern falls outside narrow norms.  
**Where used in paper:** Section 3 (Myth vs. Reality) — reframing "problem behavior" through an affirmative disability lens challenges the deficit/tragedy default.  
**Key passage for paper:** Affirmative model rejects deficit narratives in favor of recognizing difference as variation, not pathology.  
**APA:** Flynn, S. (2024). Critical disability studies and the affirmative non-tragedy model: Presenting a theoretical frame for disability and child protection. *[Journal — confirm full citation from course Drive]*.

---

### 3. Talusan (2022) — The Identity-Conscious Educator
**Chapters used:** Intro + Part 1 (Feb 2), Ch. 4 (Class, Mar 23), Ch. 7 (Disability, Apr 13)  
**Core claim:** Inclusive practice is built from repeatable habits of noticing identity, interrogating power, and acting with accountability. Ch. 4 focuses on class-based exclusion; Ch. 7 addresses ableist assumptions in school decision-making.  
**Where used in paper:**
- Section 1 (Autobiographical) — identity-conscious habits as the framework for naming your own bias
- Section 3 (Myth vs. Reality) — class + disability lens from Ch. 4 and Ch. 7
- Section 5 (Future Practice) — practical routines from Ch. 7 for avoiding ableist assumptions  
**APA:** Talusan, L. (2022). *The identity-conscious educator: Building habits and skills for a more inclusive school.* Solution Tree Press.

---

### 4. hooks (1994) — Teaching to Transgress ✅ in Course_Readings PDF
**Chapter used:** Ch. 3, "Embracing Change: Teaching in a Multicultural World" (pp. 35–44)  
**Core claim:** Real teaching requires seeing students as whole subjects. Teachers must recognize difference with compassion, not pathologize it. Engaged pedagogy requires the teacher to stay curious about the person underneath the behavior.  
**Where used in paper:** Section 4 (Assets) and Section 5 (Future Practice).  
**APA:** hooks, b. (1994). *Teaching to transgress: Education as the practice of freedom.* Routledge.

---

### 5. Gonzalez, Moll, & Amanti (2005) — Funds of Knowledge
**Core claim:** Every student brings rich experiential and cultural knowledge from home and community. Schooling's failure to recognize these funds is not student deficiency — it is a recognition failure.  
**Where used in paper:** Section 4 (Assets / Funds of Knowledge) — theoretical backbone for the asset analysis.  
**APA:** Gonzalez, N., Moll, L. C., & Amanti, C. (Eds.). (2005). *Funds of knowledge: Theorizing practices in households, communities, and classrooms.* Lawrence Erlbaum.

---

### 6. Bell (2018) — Faces at the Bottom of the Well ✅ in Course_Readings PDF
**Core claim:** Racism is not an occasional deviation but a durable, self-reproducing feature of U.S. institutions. "Progress" often masks new forms of the same structural order.  
**Where used in paper:** Section 3 background — useful for framing the structural permanence of deficit thinking in schools as a systemic pattern, not individual error.  
**Note:** Use sparingly in this paper — it is structural context, not behavioral theory. Best cited as supporting the claim that deficit labeling is institutional, not merely personal.  
**APA:** Bell, D. (2018). *Faces at the bottom of the well: The permanence of racism.* Basic Books.

---

### 7. Fine, Greene, & Sanchez (2016) — Neoliberal Blues and Prec(ar)ious Knowledge
**Core claim:** Neoliberal reforms produce precarious conditions that reshape what counts as knowledge and whose knowing is valued. Systems that assume stable resources will fail and blame students.  
**Where used in paper:** Section 3 — supports the argument that student struggle is often a product of the institutional environment (resource scarcity, austerity), not student capacity.  
**APA:** Fine, M., Greene, C., & Sanchez, S. (2016). Neoliberal blues and prec(ar)ious knowledge. *[Journal — confirm full citation from course Drive]*.

---

### 8. Desmond (2021) — "Capitalism" in The 1619 Project
**Core claim:** U.S. capitalism is structurally entangled with racial exploitation; inequality is produced, not accidental.  
**Where used in paper:** Section 3 (light background citation) — useful for grounding the claim that class-based inequity in schools is structural.  
**APA:** Desmond, M. (2021). Capitalism. In N. Hannah-Jones (Ed.), *The 1619 Project: A new origin story* (pp. 259–306). One World.
