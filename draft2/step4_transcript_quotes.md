## Section 3 — Myth vs. Reality / Observation Analysis

*These quotes are the evidentiary core. They document specific moments where student behavior that could be read as a deficit was actually a response to structural conditions.*

### 3a — Behavior as Response to Environment (not fixed trait)

| # | Quote | Source | What it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | *"I'm gonna lose it."* | Grade 4 agent challenge, Mar 12 | Student verbalization of frustration — comes **after** the task infrastructure fails (authentication issues, unclear instructions). Shows the behavioral response is contextual, not dispositional. |
| B2 | *"I don't get it. I literally don't get it."* | Grade 4 agent challenge, Mar 12 | Repeated help-seeking that precedes disengagement. Student is not refusing to learn — the support structure is not there yet. Use this to counter the myth that frustration = disinterest. |
| B3 | *"Is this about control or is it about learning? Because I'm asking you for help with a math assignment — that's about learning. You're shutting us down."* | April 6 seminar role-play debrief, ~1:49 | Instructor articulates the core tension between compliance-teaching and learning-teaching. Strong framing quote for Section 3's theoretical turn. |

### 3b — How the System Sorts Students (not ability)

| # | Quote | Source | What it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| B4 | *"The school system is designed to reward a certain type of person who thinks a certain type of way... people have to conform to what this education system wants."* | Social Class seminar, ~36 min | Structural critique of meritocracy. Use to ground the claim that 'struggling students' are often students the system was not designed for. |
| B5 | *"It may not be that one kid is just better than another. But... what we're asking them to do is second class in education."* | Social Class seminar, ~13:16 (Regents essay context) | Instructor ties test design directly to cultural capital. Very useful for arguing that assessment bias is built in, not a student deficit. |
| B6 | *"I've had a lot of teachers be like, 'Oh, you know, this is an AP class, you should know better.' Or in the non-AP class: 'You know, the AP class would go over this, we're going to skip it.'"* | Social Class seminar, ~48:19 | Everyday tracking microaggression. Documents how teachers reproduce class stratification through casual language. |
| B7 | *"Students who need that support don't receive it because there's physical boundaries between those AP classes and other classes."* | Social Class seminar, ~1:01 | Direct statement about how space and structure determine who gets support. Pair with B6. |
